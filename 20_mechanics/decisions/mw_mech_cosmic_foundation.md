# MW Mechanical Foundation — Cosmic Frame

**Status:** Decisions landed. First foundational layer of the
Mystic Weave mechanical design.

**Purpose:** Establish the physical/cosmological substrate the
rest of the world model rests on. This document does not specify
the planet's surface, biomes, climate logic, or any layer above
the cosmos itself. It commits to enough cosmological structure
that downstream design (calendar, ritual cadence, sky state,
celestial events, magic anchored to cosmic rhythm) has firm
ground to build on without being over-specified.

The intent is foundation, not exhaustive lore. Anything not
load-bearing for mechanical design is deliberately omitted.

---

## Scope

In scope:

- The star, its general type and behavior.
- The home planet, in physical terms that affect downstream
  mechanics (gravity, atmosphere, day length, year length,
  seasons).
- The moon system, including its mythological/cosmological role.
- The calendar that derives from the above.
- The Cataclysm — the cosmological event that shapes deep
  history and the magical substrate of the world.

Out of scope (deferred):

- Other planets in the system. Sketched only.
- Specific stellar age, mass, exact spectral subtype.
- Detailed moon physical properties (specific size, appearance,
  surface).
- Planet-scale surface geography, biomes, ocean/continent
  structure, climate zones.
- Sky-state mechanical surface (what the system tracks about the
  visible sky moment-to-moment).

---

## The Star

**Type:** K-type main-sequence star. An orange dwarf.

**Why it matters mechanically:** Establishes the parent body
the planet's year is measured against. K-type stars are stable,
long-lived, and slightly cooler than the sun, giving a sky that
reads slightly warmer/redder than Earth's. Habitable-zone orbits
around K stars are tighter than around G stars (Sol-type), which
is consistent with the planet's specifications below.

Galactic position, age, and mass are unspecified. None of these
are load-bearing. Fill in if and when something downstream
requires them.

---

## The Planet

**Class:** Earth-similar. Approximately 1.0–1.1 Earth radii,
approximately 1.0–1.1 G surface gravity. Density and composition
similar enough to Earth that human-scale physical intuition
transfers without translation.

**Required properties** (load-bearing for the world model):

- Plate tectonics, with the geological consequences that follow:
  mountain ranges, volcanism, earthquakes, mineral wealth.
- Active magnetic field, providing protection from stellar wind
  and supporting the atmosphere over geological time.
- Surface composition: substantial oceans plus exposed
  continents.
- Atmosphere: nitrogen-dominated with significant oxygen
  content. Earth-similar at human-scale.
- Stable orbit at low eccentricity, within the K-star habitable
  zone.
- Axial tilt sufficient to produce four distinct seasons.
- 24-hour day-night cycle, near enough to Earth that no
  retranslation of hourly time is needed.

**What this commits the design to:**

Ordinary human-scale physics works. A character who falls hits
the ground at expected speed. Water boils, fires burn, weather
patterns recognize themselves. The planet is not a physics
puzzle; it is a stage with familiar rules.

The presence of plate tectonics and a magnetic field is a frame
constraint: the world has the geological and atmospheric
dynamism that supports complex life and a layered surface
geography. This becomes input for the next foundational layer
(planet surface).

## The Year

**Length:** 364 days base. 365 days every fourth year (the leap
year).

**Average:** 364.25 days exactly. This is the planet's true
orbital period. Because the world is fictional, the period is
defined to land cleanly on the four-year cycle and does not
require the long-term corrections Earth's calendar requires
(Earth's year is 365.2422 days, which forces the
"skip leap day every 100 years, restore every 400" rule). This
world's calendar is exact at the four-year scale.

**Structure:**

- **12 months of 30 days each.** Three months per season; the
  twelve divides cleanly into the four-season cycle.
- **4 intercalary days,** one each at the spring equinox,
  summer solstice, autumn equinox, and winter solstice. These
  are not part of any month. They are days outside the months,
  occupying the four hinges of the solar year.
- **A 5th intercalary day** (the leap day) is granted every
  fourth year at the minor lunar swap (see *The Moons* below),
  bringing the leap year to 365 days.

**Why this shape:**

The 12 × 30 + 4 structure was chosen over alternatives because:

- Twelve months map cleanly to four seasons (three months each).
- The four intercalary days have intrinsic meaning — they mark
  the cardinal solar events, not arbitrary calendar artifacts.
- The structure leaves natural festival anchors at the
  equinoxes and solstices, which are mythologically dense by
  construction.
- The leap-day mechanism is unified with the lunar swap cycle,
  binding calendar correction to the cosmological event that
  defines century-scale time (see below).

**What this commits the design to:**

- A real calendar with 12 named months, 4 named seasonal
  festival days, and a recurring quadrennial festival day.
- Festivals at solstices and equinoxes as load-bearing
  worldbuilding fixtures.
- A four-year ritual cycle culminating in the minor swap
  festival.

Specific month names, season names, and festival names are
deferred. The structure is what's load-bearing; the names are
not.

## The Moons

**Configuration:** Twin co-orbital moons. Two bodies sharing a
single orbit around the planet, traveling at slightly different
relative speeds, periodically approaching each other and
exchanging orbital positions through gravitational interaction
without colliding.

This is a real orbital phenomenon. Saturn's Janus and Epimetheus
exhibit it. The moons never actually collide — gravitational
exchange reshuffles their orbits at each close approach and they
recede from each other again until the next cycle.

**Why this configuration:**

The draconic creation myth holds that the universe was brought
into existence when the two primal dragons collided. Mythological
extensions hold that another such collision would end the world.

The twin co-orbital configuration enacts this myth in the sky on
a fixed schedule. The moons approach. Tension builds. They pass
each other at a near-collision that does not happen. The cosmos
itself performs the creation myth, repeatedly, predictably,
without ever destroying the world. The duality is visible
overhead at all times.

**Cadence — Two Scales:**

- **Minor swap.** Every fourth year. The standard swap event.
  Marked by a calendar festival. Grants the 5th intercalary day
  of that year (the leap day).
- **Major swap.** Every twenty-fifth minor swap — once per
  century, exactly. A more dramatic close approach with a
  longer build-up period. Generation-defining; most adults
  witness one or two in a lifetime.

The 4-year cadence aligns with the calendar's leap correction.
The 100-year cadence aligns with the realm's century-scale
institutional cadence — most notably the Draconic Conclave,
which canon describes as gathering "approximately every century."
The Conclave is not formally triggered by the major swap (it is
called by Council decree), but the cosmic rhythm sets the natural
clock against which century-scale civic and ritual life is
measured.

**Mythological alignment:**

The War of the Fallen ended at a major swap. The Oath of the
Fallen was sworn at the cosmic hinge. This is not arbitrary —
the founders chose peace at the moment the cosmos most viscerally
enacted the creation-from-collision myth. The Oath echoes the
original draconic collision deliberately: a binding sworn under
the sky's reenactment of creation itself.

This loads founding events with cosmological weight without
requiring the cosmos to be supernatural. The orbital mechanics
are real; the mythology is the world's interpretation of them.

**What this commits the design to:**

- Two visible moons in the sky at all times, identifiable as a
  pair.
- A predictable four-year cosmic cycle, with build-up and
  release phases.
- A predictable century cycle, layered against the four-year
  cycle.
- Recurring festivals at minor swaps; rare, mythologically
  heavy festivals at major swaps.
- Anomalies in the swap cycle are meaningful — a delayed swap,
  an unusually close pass, an apparent collision read as cosmic
  disturbance.
- Twin lunar cycles, slightly out of phase, available for ritual
  and tide mechanics if downstream design wants them.

Specific moon names, sizes, surface descriptions, and visual
characteristics are deferred.

## Deep Cosmology — Before the World

Established draconic lore holds that the world itself was brought
into existence by a primal collision: two opposing forces — *the
Two* — collided, and from their collision the world was. The Two
are not deities in the modern theological sense. They are the
deepest cosmological substrate, the prior condition the world
emerged from. Every later cosmology rests on top of this.

This collision is **prior to** the Cataclysm. The Cataclysm came
later, immeasurably later, into a world the Two had already
shaped. Whether the Two themselves were present at the
Cataclysm, witnessed it, caused it, or merely echoed in it is
unsettled and unsettlable in canon.

**What this commits the design to:**

- The twin co-orbital moons are read in-world as a recurring
  reenactment of the Two's collision. The moons are not the
  Two themselves; they are the cosmos's continued performance
  of the founding pattern.
- Mythological extensions hold that another such collision
  would end the world. Each lunar swap is the cosmos
  approaching that threshold and stepping back from it.
- Pre-Cataclysm dragons existed before the Cataclysm but after
  the Two — dragons are post-world, not primordial.
- The duality (positive/negative, opposing forces in balance)
  visible in draconic religion traces directly to this deep
  cosmology.

The Two are not invoked, prayed to, or worshipped. They are
acknowledged as the cosmological condition the world rests on.
This is established backstory the eventual theology and magic
layers will rest on.

## The Cataclysm

**Origin:** Extra-solar.

A great celestial body crossed into the system from outside it,
broke against the world, and fragmented. Its fragments fell
across the world; one of the largest struck the mountain that
became the Hollow Crown. The wider Cataclysm seeded transformative
magic across the world and carried strange powers and beings in
its wake — the elves' deepest tradition holds that they were
carried in that wake, beings of starlight steadying life in the
long age that followed.

**Magic before and after the Cataclysm.** Before the strike, the
world was largely unenchanted. Dragons existed and were already
ancient, but they were great beasts with only minor magical
ability — a hot breath, a hide that resisted blade, an unease in
the air around them. There were no fields, no embodiments, no
sovereign mastery. Magic in its present form — structured,
embodied, expressible through the **nine fields** — entered the
world or woke within it at the moment of the strike. Whether the
ruinous star brought magic from outside, or whether its impact
shocked something already sleeping in the world's bones, is not
settled in canon and is not settlable.

After the Cataclysm, dragons became vessels for the new magical
spectrum. Each dragon's manifestation is singular; no two who
embody the same field do so the same way. A dragon's maturity is
measured by the breadth of its embodiment — young dragons command
one field, adults two, ancients three. The full spectrum of nine
fields exists across the totality of mature draconic embodiment.

**Why extra-solar rather than intra-system:**

- Preserves both moons intact (the moons predate the Cataclysm
  and were not disturbed by it).
- Provides a natural origin for the elves consistent with
  existing canon ("beings of pure starlight caught in the wake
  of the asteroid").
- Frames magic as something *introduced* to the world by an
  external event, rather than something native to the planet.
  This is a strong handle for downstream magic-system design.
- Establishes the Cataclysm as a singular historical event
  rather than ongoing — the wake has ended, the celestial body
  is gone, what remains is its consequences.

**What this commits the design to:**

- Magic exists because of the Cataclysm. Whatever the magic
  system turns out to be, it is anchored to this event and
  expressed through the nine fields.
- The pre/post-Cataclysm distinction in dragons (great beasts
  with minor magic before, embodied vessels of the nine fields
  after) is canon. The magic system layer must accommodate this
  history.
- The elves' origin is in the Cataclysm's wake.
- Fragments of the celestial body are scattered globally.
  The Hollow Crown is one impact site. Others exist; they are
  unspecified and available when the design needs them.
- The Cataclysm is past, not present. There is no ongoing
  celestial bombardment, no still-falling debris, no continuing
  external influence. The world has been settling from the
  event since.

## Outer System

Sketched only. The system contains other bodies — likely an
inner rocky world or two, one or more outer gas or ice giants,
possibly a debris belt or dwarf-planet zone. Specifics are
deferred. Fill in only when something downstream requires them.

The system is not empty, but it is not fully enumerated.

## What This Foundation Decides

After this layer:

- The cosmos has a defined character: a stable star, an
  Earth-class planet, a twin-moon system that performs a
  cosmological myth on a schedule, and a cataclysmic event in
  deep history that introduced magic to the world.
- The calendar is fully specified at the structural level: 12
  months of 30 days, 4 solar-hinge intercalary days, a
  quadrennial leap-day festival.
- Two scales of cosmic rhythm are available to downstream
  design: the four-year minor cycle and the century-scale major
  cycle.
- Magic has a cosmological origin, not a planetary one. This
  becomes input for the eventual magic-system layer.
- Earth-similar physics holds. Human-scale intuition about the
  physical world is preserved.

## What This Foundation Does Not Decide

- The names of months, seasons, festivals, moons.
- The specific physical properties of either moon (size,
  appearance, surface, atmosphere).
- The planet's surface geography at planetary scale (continents,
  oceans, biomes, climate zones).
- The planet's surface coverage outside the Hollow Crown
  (existing canon defines the Crown in detail; the rest of the
  globe is open).
- The mechanical surface for sky-state — whether and how the
  system represents what is visible in the sky moment to
  moment.
- How magic actually works. The Cataclysm is its origin; its
  shape, cost, and structure are downstream design.
- The character or behavior of the inner and outer planets in
  the system.

## Open Questions for Future Layers

These are not unresolved decisions at this layer; they are
markers for future foundational work that will rest on this
layer.

1. **Planet-scale surface model.** Continents, oceans, climate
   zones, biomes. The next foundational layer.
2. **Sky-state representation.** How the system tracks moon
   positions, phases, swap-cycle phase, season, time-of-day,
   and what of this is exposed to the narrator interface.
3. **Magic anchoring.** How magic, established as
   Cataclysm-derived here and expressed through the nine
   fields, integrates with mechanical design at the magic
   layer. Whether magical strength varies with cosmic state
   (swap proximity, lunar phase, season).
4. **Calendar-as-state.** How the calendar interacts with the
   eventual time and state models — what fires on intercalary
   days, what fires on swap festivals, how the century cadence
   interacts with long-arc and generational play.
5. **Environmental state is naturalistic.** When the
   environmental state layer is designed, weather, season,
   storms, and climate operate by natural causes — they are not
   coupled to the emotional state of dragons or any other
   entity. Older worldbuilding to that effect is dropped.
   Environmental drama is real but mundane in cause.
6. **The Unchanged.** A figure exists in the world's folk
   theology, called by many contradictory names across
   cultures, whose nature is change itself — thresholds,
   doorways, the cusp between old and new. Not a formal deity,
   not a worshipped god, not part of any temple structure. It
   exists as folk practice: coins at thresholds, offerings at
   gates, words spoken at crossings. The Council does not speak
   of it; the Codex of Remembrance does not name it. This is
   established lore the eventual theology and possibly
   threshold/liminal-mechanics layers will rest on.

These are referenced here so they don't get forgotten when later
layers are designed.
