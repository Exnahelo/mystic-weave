# MW Mechanical Foundation — Planet Macro-Geography

**Status:** Decisions landed. Second foundational layer of the
Mystic Weave mechanical design.

**Purpose:** Establish the planet's surface at the macro scale —
continents, oceans, climate bands, tectonic logic, and the
distribution of peoples — to the depth required for downstream
design to have hooks. Not exhaustive lore. Specific kingdoms,
cultures, polities, and named regions outside the Hollow Crown
are deferred.

Builds on `mw_mech_cosmic_foundation.md`. The planet's physical
class, calendar, and the Cataclysm framing established there are
input here.

---

## Scope

In scope:

- Number and rough arrangement of continents
- The Hollow Crown's location on the globe
- Major ocean structure
- Climate-band logic
- Tectonic state at deep-history level (post-supercontinent
  breakup)
- Where the major peoples of the world emerged from, in
  geographic terms

Out of scope (deferred):

- Specific continental coastlines, mountain shapes, river systems
- Named kingdoms, polities, cities outside Drakenvale canon
- Cultures, languages, religions of non-Drakenvale peoples
- Detailed regional geography below the continental scale
- Plate-by-plate tectonic enumeration
- Specific Cataclysm fragment sites beyond the Hollow Crown
- The character or detail of unexplored / undiscovered landmasses

---

## Approach

Geography is derived, not drawn. The planet has plate tectonics
(established in the cosmic frame), and the surface logic follows
from tectonic state, latitude, ocean configuration, and the
Cataclysm's deep-historical impact.

The planet is in a **post-supercontinent breakup phase** — the
present continents are fragments of an older whole that broke
apart in deep history. This is geological backstory, not active
state. It explains:

- Why coastlines may suggest ancient fits without literally
  puzzling together
- Why peoples across the world share elements of ancient
  ancestry
- Why mountain ranges, mineral provinces, and rift seas exist
  where they do

Active plate motion continues but slowly. The world is not in
the middle of a tectonic cataclysm.

---

## Surface Composition

- **Ocean coverage:** roughly 65–70% of the surface
- **Land coverage:** roughly 30–35%
- **Major landmasses:** two continents, one major equatorial
  archipelago

The remaining ocean is large — particularly along the planet's
exterior wraps where neither continent reaches. This is
deliberate. It preserves space for unexplored or rarely-reached
landmasses to exist if downstream design wants them. The world
is not fully mapped from the player's perspective.

---

## Continental Layout

### Northern Continent

A large temperate-to-subarctic landmass occupying the northern
hemisphere, sprawling roughly between mid-northern latitudes and
the arctic.

**Tectonic character:** Old continental shield interior with
ancient eroded mountains along its spine. Newer collision and
subduction features along its margins, particularly where it
meets the equatorial plate boundary.

**Climate range, north to south:**
- Far north: arctic seas and the Hollow Crown's outer mountain
  mass
- Subarctic: taiga, boreal forest, cold steppe
- Northern temperate: temperate forest, mixed woodland, river
  valleys
- Mid-temperate: fertile midlands, broad plains, cultivated
  basins
- Southern margin: warm temperate, transitioning toward the
  equatorial belt

**Role in design:** This is the high-fantasy core continent.
Most established lore is here. Drakenvale is at its far
northern tip.

---

### Southern Continent

A landmass occupying the southern hemisphere, opposite the
Hollow Crown along the planet's polar axis. Smaller and more
isolated than the Northern Continent.

**Tectonic character:** A tectonically active margin with
volcanic zones along part of its coastline. An older interior
with arid plateaus and continental basins. One or more rupture-
scarred regions where Cataclysm fragments struck most severely
and healing was weakest.

**Climate range, north to south:**
- Northern coast: warm temperate to subtropical, transitioning
  from the equatorial belt
- Interior: deep arid — desert and dry steppe — produced by
  continental interior climate logic plus the weak healing in
  this hemisphere
- Volcanic margins: harsh, geothermally active, mineral-rich,
  hostile
- Far southern reaches: cold steppe, glaciated highlands, polar
  margin

**Role in design:** This is the survival and harsh-frontier
continent. Healing from the elven sacrifice dispersed weakly
here — the further from the elven cradle sites, the less the
world recovered. This is the geographic anchor for the orc
homeland and weighted Vulkar presence. It is also where polar
and deep-desert gameplay live.

---

### Equatorial Archipelago

A broad equatorial belt of tropical seas, dotted with island
chains, archipelagos, and a major peninsular extension reaching
out from one of the continents.

**Tectonic character:** An island-arc system formed at oceanic
plate boundaries, with subduction-driven volcanism, deep
trenches, and frequent seismic activity. The peninsular jungle
extension is continental margin lowered by tectonic flexure.

**Climate:** Tropical. Equatorial rainforest on landmasses;
monsoon belts at the archipelago's outer edges; coral seas
between island chains.

**Major features:**
- A large, navigable archipelago dense enough to support
  maritime cultures, sea trade, and the kind of geography that
  makes piracy structurally viable
- A peninsular jungle extending from one continent into the
  equatorial zone — deep, layered, dense, the kind of terrain
  that supports exploration and survival gameplay distinct from
  arid survival
- Volcanic islands, hot springs, geothermal activity
- Coral reefs, fisheries, monsoon weather

**Role in design:**
- All north-south trade between the continents passes through
  here, making the archipelago a structural choke point
- Provides the gameplay shape for maritime / seafaring /
  pirate-style play
- Provides the gameplay shape for deep-jungle exploration and
  survival
- Cultural and political fragmentation by default — the
  archipelago is many places, not one

---

### The Inland Sea

Between the Northern and Southern Continents, partly bounded by
the equatorial archipelago to one side, lies a major inland or
rift sea — substantially enclosed by land but connected to the
world ocean through controlled openings.

**Origin:** Formed by continental rifting during the
post-supercontinent breakup. The sea sits over the rift, with
its shores marking the ancient line of separation.

**Character:** Calm relative to the open ocean. Fertile coasts.
A natural trade engine connecting both continents and the
archipelago without the open-ocean travel requirement.

**Role in design:** A Mediterranean-analogue without copying the
Mediterranean. Dense coastal civilization potential. Contested
chokepoints where the sea narrows. Deep history — drowned
ruins, ancient harbor cities, mineral wealth from the rift
process. None of this is enumerated; the sea exists, and the
narrative space exists for it to fill in when needed.

---

## Ocean Structure

- **The World Ocean.** A single dominant ocean wrapping the
  exterior of the planet — the deep, open waters that lie
  beyond the continents and archipelago. Vast, navigable in
  principle, only partly explored. Holds room for additional
  landmasses, island chains, or sea features that downstream
  design may introduce.
- **The Inland Sea.** As described above, the rift-formed
  enclosed sea between the continents.
- **Polar seas.** Arctic seas wrap the Hollow Crown's northern
  extreme. Antarctic seas wrap the southern continent's polar
  margin. Both are substantially ice-bound for portions of the
  year.

The exterior World Ocean is where unexplored geography lives.
Other continents, lost islands, rumored landmasses, sailor
legends — all available, none specified.

## Climate Bands

Standard latitude-driven climate logic, modified by ocean
positioning, mountain rain shadows, and the healing gradient
(which weakens climate stability in the southern hemisphere
interior).

| Latitude | Climate |
|---|---|
| Polar (both poles) | Ice cap / sea ice, polar tundra |
| Subarctic / subantarctic | Boreal forest, cold steppe, glaciated highlands |
| Mid-latitude (temperate) | Temperate forest, grassland, river basins |
| Subtropical | Warm temperate, drier interiors, monsoon margins |
| Tropical | Rainforest, monsoon, coral seas |
| Desert belts (~20–35° latitudes, dry continental interior) | Arid, especially severe in the southern continent's interior |

The Northern Continent's climate is broadly Earth-temperate.
The Southern Continent skews harsher than its latitude alone
would predict — the weak healing in this hemisphere left less
ecological resilience, producing more extreme deserts, harsher
volcanic regions, and more difficult winters than equivalent
latitudes on the Northern Continent.

---

## The Hollow Crown's Position

The Hollow Crown sits at the **far northern tip of the Northern
Continent**, in the arctic margin.

- North of the Crown: arctic sea, ice-bound for much of the
  year. The mountain mass is the northernmost land before the
  ice.
- A narrow rim of land surrounds the Crown's outer slopes — not
  a major peripheral region, but enough for approach paths,
  isolated outposts, and the buffer between the impossible
  mountain and open ocean.
- South of the Crown: the Northern Continent proper begins,
  sprawling southward through subarctic taiga, then temperate
  forests, then fertile midlands, eventually reaching the
  equatorial belt at its southern margin.

This placement does several things:

- Makes the Hollow Crown geographically remote — far from the
  inland sea's trade engine, far from the equatorial belt, far
  from most populated land
- Reinforces the secrecy of the sanctuary — there is genuinely
  little to find in this direction
- Aligns the directional cosmology: the Hollow Crown at the
  far north anchors the healing gradient at its strongest pole
- Establishes "the cold north, the warm south" as a natural
  directional logic for the Northern Continent
- Places the Crown roughly opposite the orc homeland on the
  Southern Continent, satisfying the canonical opposition
  between the realm of the elven cradle and the lands healed
  weakest

## The Healing Gradient

Established lore: when the star-borne beings spent their
celestial nature to heal the wounded world, healing pooled
deepest where they gathered, and weakened with distance.

This is treated as a real geographic gradient on the planet's
surface, not a metaphor:

- **Maximum healing:** the elven cradle sites, particularly the
  Heartwood within the Feywood, located in the temperate forest
  bordering the Hollow Crown. Other cradle sites exist on the
  Northern Continent, specific locations unspecified.
- **Strong healing:** the Northern Continent broadly. Fertile
  lands, balanced climate, ecological resilience.
- **Moderate healing:** the equatorial belt. Healed enough to
  be biologically rich; the Cataclysm's residue remains visible
  in volcanic island chains, anomalous reefs, and magic-rich
  geological scars.
- **Weak healing:** the Southern Continent. Climates harsher
  than latitude alone predicts. Greater density of rupture-
  scarred and corrupted regions. Bloodlines shaped by harder
  conditions.
- **Almost no healing:** the deepest interior of the Southern
  Continent — the orc homeland. Lands the healing barely
  reached. Survival cultures, hard climates, low ecological
  carrying capacity.

This gradient is not just lore color. It explains:

- Where each people emerged from
- Why the world feels divided into "the healed lands" and "the
  hard lands"
- Why magic, while global, is distributed unevenly
- Why some regions remain dangerous in ways geology alone
  doesn't account for

## Distribution of Peoples

These are origins and concentrations, not exclusive territories.
Peoples have spread across the world over the ages between the
Cataclysm and the present.

- **Humans.** The root people. Distributed broadly across both
  continents and the equatorial belt. No single homeland; the
  Cataclysm's survivors rebuilt across the world.
- **Elves.** Concentrated near the elven cradle sites where
  healing pooled deepest. The Feywood is the primary cradle
  associated with Drakenvale. Other cradles, and other elven
  realms, exist on the Northern Continent. Specific sites
  unspecified.
- **Dwarves.** Survivors who endured the Cataclysm by going
  below the earth. Distributed through major mountain ranges
  and underground networks across both continents. Strong
  presence in the Northern Continent's old eroded spine.
- **Halflings.** Concentrated in fertile midlands of the
  Northern Continent, the well-healed agricultural regions.
- **Gnomes.** Concentrated in crystalline highlands and
  magically-residued zones. Scattered, but weighted toward the
  Northern Continent.
- **Drakari.** Where draconic lineages settled. Drakenvale is
  the major modern concentration. Smaller drakari communities
  exist near other ancient draconic sites.
- **Orcs.** Southern Continent's deep interior — the
  least-healed lands. Their bloodlines are humanity shaped by
  generations of survival under conditions other peoples did
  not endure.
- **Vulkar.** Scattered across the world's rupture-scarred
  sites, weighted toward the Southern Continent's volcanic and
  geologically corrupted regions.

Specific cities, kingdoms, federations, and named cultural
groups are deferred. What's load-bearing here is geographic
origin and broad concentration.

## Cataclysm Geography

The Cataclysm was extra-solar (cosmic frame). Fragments of the
ruinous star fell across the world during the strike, scattered
by the wake's chaotic passage rather than along a directed
trajectory.

- **The Hollow Crown.** Primary impact. Where the largest
  fragment struck and lodged.
- **Other fragment sites.** Globally scattered. Some are
  Cataclysm scars — crater lakes, glass deserts, warped mineral
  provinces, magic-rich impact ejecta fields, anomalous
  geological zones. Specific sites unspecified.
- **The healing gradient.** Strongest at the elven cradle sites,
  weakening with distance. This is itself a global geographic
  feature — the surface still bears the unevenness of the
  healing.
- **No active corridor.** The Cataclysm is past. No ongoing
  bombardment, no still-falling debris. What remains is
  geological residue and the magical substrate the strike
  introduced.

Specific Cataclysm sites can be introduced when downstream
design needs them. The framing is: such sites exist globally,
they are well-known to scholars, most people have heard of one
or two, and they remain mythologically and magically charged.

## What This Foundation Decides

After this layer:

- The planet has a defined surface shape: two continents, one
  equatorial archipelago, a major inland sea, a dominant world
  ocean.
- The Hollow Crown's geographic context is established — far
  northern tip of the Northern Continent, isolated by arctic
  sea and continental sprawl.
- A directional axis exists: north (Hollow Crown, max healing)
  to south (orc homeland, weakest healing).
- Climate variety is committed — polar, temperate, tropical,
  desert, volcanic, all available without enumeration.
- Tectonic logic is established at the level needed to justify
  mountain ranges, volcanic regions, and rift features.
- The healing gradient is a real geographic feature, not just
  lore color.
- Peoples have geographic origins consistent with established
  canon and the healing gradient.
- The world ocean exterior holds room for unexplored landmasses
  and undiscovered geography.

## What This Foundation Does Not Decide

- Specific continental coastlines, mountain ranges by name,
  rivers, climate region boundaries.
- Names of continents, oceans, the inland sea, the archipelago.
- Specific cities, polities, kingdoms, federations outside
  Drakenvale canon.
- Specific elven cradle sites other than the Feywood.
- Specific Cataclysm fragment sites other than the Hollow Crown.
- Specific cultures, languages, religions of non-Drakenvale
  peoples.
- Trade route specifics, political relationships between
  regions, cross-continental diplomatic structure.
- The exact tectonic plate configuration.
- The character or location of any unexplored landmasses in
  the world ocean.

## Open Questions for Future Layers

1. **Spatial model — multi-scale containment.** How the system
   represents nested places (planet → continent → region →
   settlement → district → structure → room). How non-Euclidean
   pockets (Feywood) attach to the Euclidean base. How
   underground layers stack.
2. **Environmental state per region.** What state each region
   carries (climate, season, hazards, ambient magic, healing
   gradient effect). How that state varies with time.
3. **Travel and movement at planetary scale.** Cross-continental
   travel, sea travel, the gameplay-relevant difficulty of
   reaching different zones.
4. **Cataclysm-site mechanics.** When other fragment sites are
   introduced, what makes them mechanically distinct from
   ordinary geography.
5. **Healing gradient as mechanical state.** Whether and how
   the gradient affects ambient magic, encounter difficulty,
   resource availability, or other downstream mechanics.

These are markers for future layers, not unresolved decisions
at this layer.
