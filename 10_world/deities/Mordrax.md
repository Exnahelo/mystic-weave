# Mordrax

## Overview

Mordrax is among the oldest known deities of the planet — the cosmic principle of dissolution, change-without-restraint, and the unmaking of form. She is one half of a primal polarity whose other half is [[Solveris]], and the conflict between them is not a struggle of evil against good but the fundamental dynamic by which the world holds and changes. The [[Codex of Remembrance]] frames Mordrax as corruption, ambition unbound, and the danger the sanctuary exists to refuse — and that framing is real and binding for civic life — but it is interpretation. What Mordrax is, beneath the mortal frame, is the pole that unmakes.

Like [[Solveris]], Mordrax was worshipped by dragons before the [[Cataclysm]]. The [[Temple of Mordrax]] beneath the [[Shadowed Hollows]] predates the sanctuary because the religion predates the realm.

## Domains

The mortal-civic framings commonly attached to Mordrax in [[Drakenvale]]:

- Corruption
- Ambition unbound
- Power pursued without restraint
- That which the [[Codex of Remembrance]] exists to refuse

These are how Drakenvale's civic religion articulates the dissolution-pole within a moral order built on the opposing pole. At the cosmic level, Mordrax is dissolution itself — the unmaking-of-form that exists as one half of the dynamic by which the world is. Mortal civic religion in [[Drakenvale]] receives that as moral threat because the sanctuary's order is built on Solveris's pole; mortal religion in other places may receive it differently.

## Origin and scope

Mordrax-worship, like [[Solveris]]-worship, is far older than the sanctuary. The polarity between them is the dragons' oldest religious understanding, predating the [[Cataclysm]]. Mortal civilizations across the planet inherited the framework through draconic-derived faith.

The [[Temple of Mordrax]] beneath the [[Shadowed Hollows]] was here when the founders arrived. They did not build it; they inherited it. Whoever first articulated dragon-religion through built structure for the dissolution-pole left no preserved record of when, why, or by whom. The temple is older than the [[Oath of the Fallen]] because the religion is older than the realm.

Outside the [[Hollow Crown]], Mordrax-worship is not necessarily hidden or forbidden. The sanctuary's prohibition is sanctuary policy — [[Drakenvale]] does not police the wider world. Societies elsewhere on the planet may openly hold Mordrax-aligned faith, particularly where the dissolution-pole is held to be the more honest face of cosmic truth. No specific such societies are currently established in canon, but their existence is plausible and not foreclosed.

The names "Solveris" and "Mordrax" themselves are of unknown origin. No one knows where they came from. The dragons have always called them by these names, and so have the mortals who learned the religion from dragons, and so does every record old enough to mention the deities at all. A religion whose own naming-history is lost is older than any preserved record.

## Worship

Forbidden within the sanctuary. The [[Codex of Remembrance]] does not forbid Mordrax-worship because Mordrax is evil — the cosmological frame holds her as one of the two oldest principles, not an evil one — but because the sanctuary chose [[Solveris]]'s pole as the foundation of its civic order, and active devotion to the opposing pole within the realm is incompatible with that choice. Active Mordrax devotion is treated as a threat to the sanctuary and is among the gravest offenses under the Codex — the only category for which the Codex permits execution by unanimous Council approval.

What persists illicitly within the sanctuary:

- [[Mordrax Cults]] — small, recurring, hard to extinguish. They surface in different forms across centuries.
- The [[Mordraxian Rebels]] — the historical faction that pursued her power openly during the [[Discordant War]]. Their attempt to unseal her temple via the [[Dark Hold]] cost the [[Platinum Warden]] his life.

## Mythology

Mordrax is the dissolution-pole to [[Solveris]]'s preservation-pole. The two are not framed as siblings, lovers, or rivals in canonical liturgy — the relationship is left philosophical rather than mythological — but the cosmic structure is clear: each is what the other is not, and both are necessary for the world to be what the world is.

The [[Codex of Remembrance]] frames their conflict in moral terms because civic order requires moral language. At a deeper frame, the conflict simply *is* the deities. They are their opposition.

## Iconography

Suppressed within the sanctuary. Public depiction of Mordrax in [[Drakenvale]] is itself suspect. Outside the realm, where Mordrax-worship is not necessarily forbidden, iconography is varied and culturally specific.

What survives in the sanctuary's restricted scholarly archives describes her as faceless, voracious, and bound — though whether the binding is physical, ritual, or metaphoric is itself contested. Theologians attentive to the cosmological frame note that "voracious" reads as a moral judgment rendered structural, and that the suppressed iconography is not necessarily the deity's most honest form.

## Open questions

Most what-is-Mordrax questions move into different shape under the new framing: a primal pole, recognized by dragons, articulated through mortal religion. What remains:

- Whether Mordrax is "a being with intent" or "a structural principle" or something stranger
- Whether [[Solveris]] and Mordrax are genuinely two distinct deities or two faces of a single underlying polarity. Liturgical convention treats them as two; the cosmological frame as a single dynamic expressed bilaterally. The Codex does not require an answer; some theologians treat the question itself as the religion's deepest mystery
- The names and lineages of the dragon-leaders of the [[Mordraxian Rebels]] — and which of those lineages still carry social distrust in the [[Modern Era]]
- The form and substance of Mordrax-worship outside the [[Hollow Crown]] — across the planet, in cultures where her pole is openly held to be canonical
- Whether dragons of the [[Draconic Council]] understand Mordrax in the cosmological frame outlined here, in the Codex's moral frame, or both
- How active her cult network is in the present age
