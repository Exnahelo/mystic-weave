# Vulkar

## Overview

The Vulkar arose in rupture-scarred and corrupted places — the regions where [[The Cataclysm]] did not heal, did not transform benignly, and did not abandon to mere hardship, but tore something open that never closed. Their cultural identity is built around inherited burden, suspicion of triumphant origin stories, and the truth that some peoples carry the cost of old breaches in their very bodies.

## Origin

Vulkar emerged from survivors in the rupture-scarred and corrupted lands left by [[The Cataclysm]]. Their bloodline traces to peoples who endured in places where the world's wound did not close — where the post-impact restoration brought corruption alongside whatever life it preserved. The Vulkar are the people who survived in those wounded lands, and the survival is written into them.

## Cataclysm memory

Vulkar generally believe [[The Cataclysm]] left wounds that never fully closed. Their traditions center on the idea that not all change was benevolent, not all magic was healing, and some bloodlines were marked by places where the world tore open in darker ways. They are likely to be skeptical of overly beautiful or triumphant versions of the origin story.

> Vulkar: some wounds never closed, and some blood still bears them.

Vulkar belief emphasizes inherited burden, survival under suspicion, the persistence of scars, and the truth that some peoples carry the cost of old breaches in their very bodies.

## Culture

Defined by carrying. Vulkar culture is shaped by the recognition that they bear something the world's other peoples do not — not as gift, not as curse exactly, but as inheritance. Vulkar tradition emphasizes endurance under suspicion (other peoples often regard Vulkar with caution because of their origin), the discipline required not to become what their wounded blood might tempt them toward, and the dignity of survival without illusion.

Vulkar diplomatic life is often cool and formal. Vulkar communities tend toward inward solidarity. Vulkar relationships with [[Solveris]]-aligned doctrine are complicated — the Vulkar have reasons to be skeptical of moral frameworks that treat their inheritance as a problem to be redeemed.

## Distribution

Across the rupture-scarred and corruption-touched regions of the world. Vulkar presence in [[Drakenvale]] is real but modest, and Vulkar relationships with the [[Codex of Remembrance]] are not always easy — the Codex's framing of corruption is something Vulkar tradition reads more carefully than other peoples.

## Magical character

Vulkar work across the [[Nine Magical Fields]] but with notable affinity for Necromancy, Binding, and the darker aspects of fields most peoples handle gingerly. This is not because the Vulkar are corrupt; it is because their inheritance gives them a closer working relationship to fields concerned with what persists past wound, what binds what should not be free, and what endures when restoration was incomplete.

Vulkar magical practitioners are sometimes regarded with suspicion by peoples who do not understand the difference between Vulkar inheritance and active corruption. The misunderstanding is itself part of what Vulkar culture has had to manage.

## Relationships with other peoples

- [[Drakenvale]] and [[Solveris]]-aligned doctrine — uneasy; the framing of corruption is contested
- [[Mordrax]] and the [[Mordraxian Rebels]] — emphatically NOT aligned. Vulkar inheritance is not Mordraxian devotion, and Vulkar communities have historically pushed back hard against the conflation. The conflation is a slander Vulkar peoples take seriously.
- [[Gnomes]] — uneasy; transformation-versus-corruption is theologically charged between them
- [[Elves]] — distant; the elven framing of [[Cataclysm]] healing as their gift sits poorly with Vulkar memory of un-healed wound
- [[Humans]], [[Halflings]], [[Dwarves]], [[Orcs]] — varies by region
