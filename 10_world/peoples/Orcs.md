# Orcs

## Overview

Orcs are the people of the lands [[The Cataclysm]] healed least. When the impact's celestial-and-elven restoration spread across the world, it did not spread evenly. Some lands flourished; some were merely steadied; some remained ruptured, scarce, and brutal. The peoples who survived in those least-healed lands became the orcs — shaped by hardship, by the truth of hunger, and by the lasting memory that mercy was rare on their side of the world.

## Origin

Orcs emerged from survivors in the lands of rupture, scarcity, and brutality where post-[[Cataclysm]] relief came late or weakly. Their origin is not a transformation but a hardening — what life required of them in the lands they were given to survive in.

## Cataclysm memory

Orcs generally believe the world was broken unevenly and healed unevenly. Their deepest traditions remember that on the far side of the world, the [[Cataclysm]] left lands of rupture, scarcity, and brutality where relief came late or weakly. In orc cultural memory, the world was not restored for everyone at once, and strength became necessary because mercy was rare.

> Orcs: the world healed unevenly, and we survived where it did not.

Orc belief emphasizes survival through hardship, the truth of hunger and conflict, distrust of peoples who speak too easily of harmony, and respect for those who endure without illusion.

Where elves remember healing, orcs remember abandonment. The world was not restored equally, and orc memory may preserve that bitterness.

## Culture

Defined by what hardship required. Orc culture is not monolithic — different orc peoples in different harsh lands developed different traditions — but shared themes recur: respect for endurance, suspicion of peoples who claim universal moral truths, loyalty within tight networks, and the conviction that comfort is something earned, never given.

Orcish religious life tends toward acknowledging powers that demand strength rather than powers that promise mercy. The contrast with [[Solveris]]-aligned [[Drakenvale]] doctrine is profound.

## Distribution

Across the harsher regions of the world — the lands the post-[[Cataclysm]] healing did not reach evenly. Orc presence within [[Drakenvale]] specifically is limited; the sanctuary's [[Codex of Remembrance]] is a doctrine that reads, from many orc perspectives, as an extension of the same uneven healing that left their lands behind.

## Magical character

Orcs work within the [[Nine Magical Fields]] when training and circumstance permit, but orc magical tradition has historically emphasized the elemental, the practical, and the bound-to-survival. Sacred-aligned magic is rarer in orcish practice; necromancy and warding are more common.

## Relationships with other peoples

- [[Humans]] — variable; long-running tensions in some regions, cooperation in others
- [[Dwarves]] — frequently tense; competing for harsh terrain
- [[Elves]] — distant and theologically opposed; the elven framing of [[Cataclysm]] sacrifice does not align with orcish memory of abandonment
- [[Drakari]] / [[Drakenvale]] — limited contact; the sanctuary's secrecy and its doctrinal alignment make sustained orc-Drakenvale relations rare
