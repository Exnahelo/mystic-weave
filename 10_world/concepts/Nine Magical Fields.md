# Nine Magical Fields

## Definition

The Nine Magical Fields are the structured forms through which magic in its present age is worked, taught, and classified. Each field is a distinct mode of magical operation with characteristic shapes, costs, and risks. The nine-fold structure is the dominant framework for understanding magic across the world, descended from draconic tradition; whether it is the true architecture of magic or simply the most widely held way of cutting it is itself disputed (see *Variants and disputes* below).

The fields became the operative shape of magic with [[The Cataclysm]]. Before the impact, dragons commanded only sparks of innate power; the structured spectrum began when the falling star struck. That magic in its present form began with the Cataclysm is settled; by what mechanism the Cataclysm produced it is disputed and catalogued at [[Origin of Magic]].

## The Nine Fields

- **Sacred** — the magic of consecration, judgment, blessing, and protection
- **Warding** — the magic of containment, boundary, threshold, and defense
- **Binding** — the magic of oath, fixation, anchoring, and constraint
- **Elemental** — the raw forces of fire, water, air, earth, and storm
- **Alchemy** — the magic of transformation through composition
- **Runecraft** — the magic of inscribed power, durable enchantment, and structural working
- **Druidry** — the magic of life, growth, the wild, and natural cycles
- **Necromancy** — the magic of death, persistence past death, and what remains
- **Illusion** — the magic of appearance, perception, and what is shown versus what is

## Scope

The fields are universal across the world — every magical practitioner works within one or more of them, regardless of ancestry. They are not the only way magic can be expressed (idiosyncratic, accidental, or inherited workings exist outside formal field structure), but they are the dominant framework through which magic is taught, classified, and contested.

## Mechanics in the world

Each [[Dragon|dragon]] embodies one or more fields innately, and a dragon's maturity is measured by the breadth of that embodiment:

- A young dragon naturally commands one field
- An adult dragon naturally commands two fields
- An ancient dragon naturally commands three fields

Each dragon's manifestation of an embodied field is unique — no two dragons who embody the same field do so the same way. [[Drakari]] inherit magical affinity from draconic bloodline; what is passed down is a natural inclination toward the fields most strongly embodied by the dragon in that bloodline, not a fixed universal expression.

For mortal practitioners, fields are learned rather than embodied. Mastery in a field requires study, practice, and (in the case of dangerous fields) institutional authorization. The [[Hall of Scales]] handles foundational education across fields; the [[Arcane Conservatory]] handles advanced specialization.

## Distribution among ancient dragons

The full spectrum of nine fields is expressed across the ancient draconic order through embodied magical constellations. Each ancient dragon commands three fields:

- [[Eryndor the Radiant]] — Sacred, Warding, Binding
- [[Zarkeros the Inferno]] — Elemental, Alchemy, Runecraft
- [[Varethyn of the Amethyst Gaze]] — Druidry, Necromancy, Illusion
- [[Ardrynn the Measured]] — Sacred, Alchemy, Druidry

Note that fields are not exclusive — multiple dragons can embody the same field. [[Eryndor the Radiant]] and [[Ardrynn the Measured]] both embody Sacred; [[Zarkeros the Inferno]] and [[Ardrynn the Measured]] both embody Alchemy; [[Varethyn of the Amethyst Gaze]] and [[Ardrynn the Measured]] both embody Druidry.

## Variants and disputes

The origin of the fields' magic — how the Cataclysm produced it — is not argued here; that dispute lives at [[Origin of Magic]]. The dispute proper to the fields is different: whether the nine-fold structure is the true architecture of magic, or the dominant *classification* of it.

The nine fields are draconic in origin — the way dragons embody magic, and the way draconic-derived institutions teach it — and that framing has become near-universal through Drakenvale's reach and the authority of the [[Hall of Scales]] and [[Arcane Conservatory]]. But a classification is not the thing classified. The underlying magic is one substrate; the nine fields are the dominant way of cutting it, not necessarily the only true one. Other traditions may divide the same magic differently — fewer categories, more, or along entirely different seams — and hold their own cut to be the more honest.

This is a dispute of map, not of mechanics. A working behaves the same regardless of which tradition's name is laid over it; what varies is how cultures bound, group, and teach the same underlying power. No society currently established in canon practices an alternative classification, but the nine-field framework is not foreclosed as the only possible one — the space for divergent magical traditions is deliberately left open.

- Whether non-draconic beings have ever embodied fields innately
- Whether new fields could emerge or existing fields disappear
- The exact mechanism of inheritance for [[Drakari]] — affinity is passed, but with what variability?
- Whether any non-draconic tradition's classification of magic will enter canon, and how its categories would map onto the nine fields
