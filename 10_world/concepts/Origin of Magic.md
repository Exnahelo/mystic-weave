# Origin of Magic

## Definition

The Origin of Magic is the contested question of how the magic of the
present age came to be. It is one of the world's oldest unresolved disputes,
and — by design — it has no canonical answer. Different traditions hold
different origins, and as more peoples and civilizations enter the world's
history, more positions accrete. This note is the canonical home for that
dispute; other notes describe the event and the forms of magic, but the
disagreement over its origin lives here.

## What is settled

The *timing* is not disputed in serious histories. Before [[The Cataclysm]],
dragons commanded only sparks of innate power, humans had no meaningful
magic, and the world was largely unenchanted. When the ruinous star fell,
magic in its present world-shaping form began: structured, transmissible,
embodied, and expressible through what later traditions classify as the
[[Nine Magical Fields]].

The Cataclysm is the hinge; what it did is the dispute.

(The collision of [[The Two]] that made the world is a separate and far
earlier event. It is the world's origin, not magic's. The two are not
conflated.)

## What is contested

What the Cataclysm *did* is the open question. No serious tradition denies
that magic in its present form began with the Cataclysm, but traditions
disagree over whether the falling star brought magic, awakened it, wounded
the world deeply enough for it to leak through, or provided the occasion
for other powers to act.

The positions currently established in canon:

- **Carried in (celestial intrusion).** Magic was foreign to the world,
  borne by the falling star and its wake — received as gift or as
  contamination depending on the teller.
- **Awakened (native latency).** Magic was always latent in the world's
  bones; the impact did not bring it but shocked it awake, stripping away a
  silence that had hidden it.
- **Released through the wound (breach).** The impact tore the world open
  deeply enough that magic seeped through from somewhere the intact world
  had held shut — neither carried in nor simply native, but loosed by the
  breaking.
- **Sent by the deities (draconic-theistic strand).** [[Solveris]] and
  [[Mordrax]] — the Two — sent the magic by divine will. For adherents the
  question is closed: the deities are the answer, and the Cataclysm only the
  instrument.
- **Shaped by elven sacrifice.** The star-borne elves, spending their
  celestial nature to steady the dying world (see [[Elves]]), gave the new
  magic its form and character — the impact loosed raw power, but what it
  became owes to that outpouring.

The elven tradition's *own* account of magic's origin is a separate matter,
and not yet established in canon. The bullet above is a position *about*
magic in the dispute; the elves' self-understanding of where it came from
remains open ground (see [[Elves]]).

## Why it stays open (design intent)

The origin of magic is deliberately left contested. It functions as a seam
along which religions and cultures diverge: each people that enters canon
may hold its own account, and the disagreements are the point, not a gap to
be closed. New positions are expected to be added to this note as the world
expands outward from the founding setting.

## Open questions

- The elven tradition's formal position on magic's origin.
- Whether any tradition's account is privileged by evidence, or whether all
  remain equally unprovable. (Current canon: equally unprovable.)
- Which further peoples and religions will hold which positions as the world
  expands.
