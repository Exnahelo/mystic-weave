# Misty Descent

Below the [[Wardline Threshold|wardline]], the mountain slope drops steeply through dense, rolling fog. **The temperature shifts** — alpine cold giving way to an unnatural warmth that rises from the valley below. **Visibility drops to thirty feet.** The mist is not weather; it is **the outermost influence of the [[Southwestern Mystic Wetlands|Mystic Wetlands]] bleeding upslope**. Glowing pools collect in rock hollows along the path, their water faintly restorative. **Sounds carry strangely** — footsteps echo, voices seem to come from the wrong direction. **Will-o'-wisps have been seen here**, drifting through the fog with their own alien purpose.

## Character

The Misty Descent is the **transition zone** between [[Alpine Peaks]] alpine cold and [[Southwestern Mystic Wetlands|wetland]] atmosphere. **It is unusual geography.** The wetland's atmospheric character bleeds upslope here, producing fog density and warmth that should not exist at this altitude. The realm's institutional knowledge has not fully resolved why this happens; the wetland is canonically the realm's drainage terminus, and the Misty Descent appears to be where some portion of that drainage's atmospheric output rises.

### Sensory phenomena

The Descent's character is **disorienting in specific ways**:

- **Visibility drops to thirty feet** — the fog is dense enough that travelers cannot see structures, paths, or companions beyond short range
- **Acoustic distortion** — footsteps echo where they should not, voices seem to come from wrong directions, sounds travel further or shorter than expected
- **Thermal shift** — alpine cold to unnatural warmth across a span of descent that geography alone does not account for
- **Will-o'-wisps** — small drifting lights that move through the fog with alien purpose. **They are not aggressive in the ordinary sense** — encounters are uncommon, the wisps drift without engaging — **but their presence is one of the Descent's most reliable phenomena**
- **Glowing pools** — rock hollows collect water that retains a faint glow. **The water is faintly restorative**; small quantities have been used by travelers in genuine need, though the realm's healers caution against habitual use

### Disorientation

The Descent is **disorienting beyond ordinary fog effect**. Travelers report:

- **Directional confusion** — compasses and dead reckoning grow unreliable
- **Path-finding difficulty** — even known routes seem different on each crossing
- **Time perception** — the descent feels shorter or longer than it should, with no reliable correlation to actual time elapsed

These effects are mild compared to the [[Wardline Threshold]]'s deterrence, but **they are real and they accumulate over the duration of the crossing**. Most travelers complete the Descent without serious incident; **a small fraction find themselves significantly more lost than they expected**.

## Function

- **Transition geography** between [[Alpine Peaks]] and [[Southwestern Mystic Wetlands]]
- **Wetland atmospheric outflow** — the Descent is where the wetland's character first manifests on the alpine side
- **Restorative water source** at the glowing pools, used sparingly by travelers in need
- **Will-o'-wisp habitat** — one of the realm's known wisp locations

## Travel and access

The Descent is **part of the wilderness chain** through the [[Alpine Peaks]] — connected upslope to the [[Wardline Threshold]] and downslope to [[Valley Edge Overlook]] in the [[Southwestern Mystic Wetlands]]. **Sanctioned travel does not pass through this route;** the realm's standard entry is via [[Lastmark]] in the alpine institutional zone.

Travelers who do find themselves on this route — typically those who have somehow penetrated the [[Wardline Threshold]] or are descending from external alpine wilderness on a path that does not cross [[Lastmark]] — face the disorientation, the warmth shift, the wisps, and the genuine difficulty of pathfinding. **Realm institutions do not patrol the Descent.** Local knowledge of safe passage exists at [[Mirefall]] and [[Brackenmoor]] for the rare cases where travelers from this direction reach the wetland communities.
