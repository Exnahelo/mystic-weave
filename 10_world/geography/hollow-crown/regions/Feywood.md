# Feywood

The liminal forest realm that exists within the [[Hollow Crown]]'s geography but does not obey its ordinary rules. The Feywood appears from outside as a contained patch of the western temperate forest — a pocket perhaps half a day's walk across at its widest, bordered clearly by the realm-side Feywood Border. Inside, the Feywood is larger than any realm-side geography can account for. Its forests extend in ways that are not measurable from outside. Its interior holds the elven city of [[Thornveil]] around the [[Heartwood]] that is the land's living center, the [[Silent Grove]] at the elven origin point, secondary settlements including [[Vaelmere]], sacred groves, and the wild terrain between them — all occupying space that, by any ordinary reckoning, should not fit within the Feywood's external footprint. The Feywood is not hostile. It is not corrupted. It is magical land sustained by the [[Heartwood]]'s continuous working, protected by the [[Elves]] who have lived here since before the realm existed, and by the land itself in return.

## Scene texture

Crossing the Border into the Feywood is immediate and gentle. The moment of crossing carries a shift — the change in air, the altered quality of sound, the brief passage-sensation — and then the traveler is in the Feywood. The initial terrain looks like a continuation of the western temperate forest. But within the first few minutes of walking, the geometry begins to be the Feywood's geometry rather than the realm's.

Distances do not behave consistently. A tree that appears twenty paces away may take thirty paces to reach. Another tree may be reached in fewer paces than its distance suggested. Paths that seemed to curve one way resolve into different curves when walked. The effect is disorienting for first-time visitors; it is ordinary to elves and to [[Hall of Guides]] members who know the Feywood professionally. What seems like a trick is a feature: the Feywood's geography responds to the traveler's intent and attention through the [[Heartwood]]'s continuous reading of those who cross into the field. Those who walk with respect and purpose find their way more easily than those who do not.

The forest itself is denser and older than realm-side forest. Familiar species — oaks, beeches, the broader hardwoods of the western temperate biome — grow to ancient proportions. Individual trees reach heights and trunk diameters no realm-side tree achieves. The canopy reaches staggering heights; the understory is a rich layered ecosystem with flowering vines and specimens of plant life that grow only here.

Light in the Feywood behaves with its own character. Dappled canopy light falls in patterns more complex than ordinary sun-through-leaves. Clearings hold specific qualities of light — bright and warm in some, cool and blue-green in others, a pale gold quality in a few that realm-side light does not produce. Night is not merely darkness: bioluminescent flora glows faintly throughout the understory.

Sounds are layered: birdsong from species not found in the realm, recognizable and unrecognizable animal calls, wind through canopy producing a choral effect, occasional whispers that are not from any identifiable source. Travelers learn to let those sounds pass without attending to them too closely.

The air carries a specific quality — cooler, slightly more humid, faintly sweet. Long exposure produces subtle effects on residents. Elves who live here all their lives carry the Feywood's signature in their bearing and skin quality, and long-term human residents eventually develop something of the same.

Wildlife is abundant. Deer, boar, smaller game — many shared with realm-side forest, some specific to the Feywood. Predators exist in the deeper terrain. The Feywood's wildlife is not directed by the [[Heartwood]]; the animals are not its agents. The [[Heartwood]] communicates with the broader ecosystem, and the ecosystem responds from its own nature. A wolf moves toward a disrespectful traveler as a wolf would move toward a threat in its territory, prompted by the Heartwood's reading but acting on its own pattern.

## The liminal geometry

The Feywood's most distinctive feature is its internal geometry. The bigger-on-the-inside character is sustained continuously by the [[Heartwood]] — a sentient, conscious tree at the Feywood's living center whose deeper canon is treated in [[Heartwood]].

**Internal scale.** The Feywood contains [[Thornveil]], the [[Heartwood]] at its sacred center, the [[Silent Grove]] at the elven origin point, the secondary settlement of [[Vaelmere]] at the Border-facing edge, elven noble family estates scattered through the old forest, and wild terrain between these locations. Travel between major locations takes hours or days depending on the journey and the traveler's relationship with the land. None of this fits within the Feywood's external dimensions as observed from the realm side.

**Path-finding.** Travelers moving through the Feywood with clear intent and appropriate disposition find their way. The land accommodates them. Paths appear where paths are needed. Distances become traversable. Landmarks orient. This is not magical guidance that can be deliberately invoked; it is the [[Heartwood]]'s reading of the traveler combined with the natural cooperation of a land that responds to communication.

**Displacement.** Travelers moving without clear intent, without respect, or while actively disoriented experience the opposite. Distances become uncertain. Paths do not resolve. Landmarks repeat in impossible configurations. Extended lost-wandering is genuinely dangerous — and the danger is not only navigational. The [[Heartwood]]'s roots have linked over millennia with plant root systems throughout the [[Hollow Crown]], and travelers expelled from the Feywood may emerge through that network rather than at the Border. Full canon is in [[Heartwood]] under The Root Network.

**Time.** Time in the Feywood is usually consistent with time in the realm, but not always. Short visits typically produce no time discrepancy. Longer visits sometimes do — travelers spending days in the Feywood occasionally emerge to find that more or fewer days have passed in the realm. Effects are minor; rarely more than a day or two.

## Weather and the Heartwood's health

Within the Feywood, weather is linked to the [[Heartwood]]'s health. Under healthy conditions the Feywood holds in spring and summer continuously. When the [[Heartwood]] is troubled, threatened, or stressed, the weather shifts:

- **Standing Green** — healthy default state
- **Turning** (autumn beginning) — something is wrong
- **Stillfall** (winter) — something is very wrong
- **Plant die-off** — the [[Heartwood]] is drawing on the land's life to sustain its working
- **Animal aggression** — downstream of plant die-off; ecosystem under stress

Full canon is in [[Heartwood]] under Weather as Health Signal.

## The elven homeland

The Feywood is the elves' homeland and has been since the founding. After [[The Cataclysm]], the elves of the age gave up their immortality to repair the land. The first trees of the valley rose from that sacrifice. The elves settled among those first trees, at the site that is now the [[Silent Grove]]. From that founding, the [[Elves]] have lived in the Feywood continuously. The [[Heartwood]] was discovered later, by an elf adjacent to the [[Silent Grove]] community; [[House Heartwood]] took its name from that discovery.

The elven population is the Feywood's majority demographic. Ancient noble families trace their lineage back through generations whose total span is measured in millennia.

**Trust-based residence is not species-bound.** Non-elves live in the Feywood as well, but their presence is conditional on demonstrated respect. Humans, dwarves, drakari, orcs, and members of other realm species can become Feywood residents if their conduct proves acceptable over time. Integration is not ceremonial — there is no oath-taking, no acceptance ritual — but it is real. The [[Heartwood]]'s protection extends to anyone the [[Heartwood]] trusts, regardless of species.

The elves' relationship with the Feywood is not one of ownership. The Feywood is not property; it is a place the elves live in and protect. The elves speak of being the Feywood's stewards rather than its owners.

## Governance

The Feywood is governed by a **Council of Elders** — a consensus-based body drawn from elven noble families, druidic practitioners, and recognized elder figures whose wisdom has accumulated over centuries. The Council does not have a single leader. Deliberations move at a pace that realm-side observers often find remarkable — decisions may take weeks or years.

Primary responsibilities:

- **[[Heartwood]] stewardship** — protection of the [[Heartwood]] is the Council's foremost responsibility and the reason the Council exists
- **External diplomatic coordination** — relationships with the realm, the [[Oath of the Fallen]]'s ongoing obligations, rare extra-realm contact
- **Internal dispute resolution** — disputes between settlements and noble families, matters affecting the broader elven community
- **Military coordination** — the Feywood's formal military answers to the Council
- **Resource and mercantile oversight** — managed through Council-appointed Trade Envoys

Routine Council business is conducted in the Heartwood Approach district of [[Thornveil]]. **Council convenings of land-weight** — decisions whose implications touch the Feywood itself rather than merely its institutional affairs — are held in the Council Chamber beneath the [[Heartwood]], accessed through the natural opening between the tree's buttress roots. Such convenings are rare and are documented in [[Heartwood]].

## Military structure

Three distinct martial orders, organized as parallel institutions rather than nested hierarchies. Each has a specific scope and operational profile.

### [[Heartwarden]]
Elite sacred guardians of the Feywood's inner sanctums. Primary charge: protection of the [[Heartwood]], the [[Silent Grove]], and the most sensitive druidic and ceremonial sites. Selective, rarely deployed for ordinary security work. Trained at the [[Silent Grove]] on a seven-year cycle.

### [[Greenshield]]
The main practical defense and security force. Visible day-to-day protectors of civic life, trade movement, sanctioned routes, and Border approaches. Body most people encounter in ordinary matters of safety, patrol, and order. Distributed broadly.

### [[Ranger]]
A specialized branch *within* the Greenshields focused on outer-range fieldwork, small-team operations, and low-visibility response beyond the main settlements. Rangers operate in small teams or as lone operators; mobile, discreet, information-driven. They are administratively part of the Greenshields and report through Greenshield command, but their operational profile is distinct enough that the Ranger designation is recognized as its own role within the broader order.

### Order relationships

Heartwardens and Greenshields are parallel orders — neither subordinates to the other; they coordinate. Rangers sit *within* the Greenshield structure with significant operational autonomy. Crisis deployments may bring all three together, as occurred during the [[Discordant War]] when [[Heartwarden]]s, [[Greenshield]] contingents, and [[Ranger]] scouts deployed jointly under [[Lethariel Heartwood]]'s command.

## Religion and the land

Elven religious practice is not centered on deities. It is a practice of respect for the land, the animals, the trees, the [[Heartwood]], and the deeper life the Feywood itself represents. The practice is druidic in form — seasonal observances, ceremonies tied to natural cycles, meditative and ritual practices performed at specific sites, the deep study of the Feywood's ecology and magical character as spiritual knowledge rather than mere biology.

The [[Heartwood]] is the central sacred site, but the elven understanding of what the [[Heartwood]] actually is remains canonically multiple. Three traditions persist (remnant magic from the healing, a celestial-who-remained, an ordinary plant that collected the leftover energy), and the elves do not require a single answer. See [[Heartwood]].

The [[Silent Grove]] is the secondary sacred site and is canonically the elves' origin point in this world.

Elves speak of the land as a relationship rather than as a resource. The practice of respect — giving back what is taken, leaving what should be left, attending to what requires attention — is not a religious obligation in the ordinary sense. It is the ecological reality of living in a place that responds. Disrespect is not a religious sin; it is a practical error that the Feywood corrects.

This worldview is incompatible with the realm's [[Solveris]] monotheism in some deep philosophical senses, but the two traditions have coexisted for the full span of the realm's existence without requiring reconciliation.

## Relationship with the realm

- **[[Oath of the Fallen]] alliance.** The Feywood swore alliance to the nascent realm during the Oath's founding period. Mutual non-interference and mutual aid in true crisis. The [[Discordant War]]'s Feywood contingent was the only canonical fulfillment of the mutual-aid clause.
- **Mutual non-interference.** Each polity governs its own territory.
- **Mercantile exchange.** The primary ongoing contact, conducted at the Border. Significant for both sides.
- **Diplomatic contact.** Routes through the Council of Elders. Pace mismatch between realm and Council deliberations is real and managed.
- **Cultural distance.** Elves and realm cultures remain distinct after a millennium of proximity. The elves are not partially-assimilated realm residents; they are residents of a different polity that shares geography.

## Character

The Feywood has a specific atmospheric character that distinguishes it from every other region. It is magical at a continuous background intensity — not dramatic magical effects, but ambient magic that colors light, sound, wildlife, weather, and the traveler's own sense of self. The Feywood does not feel like a realm with magic in it; it feels like magic manifesting as a realm.

The Feywood's trickster reputation in realm folklore is partial truth. The Feywood does respond to disrespect in ways that realm visitors sometimes find cruel — lost travelers wandering in circles, promising paths that loop back, apparent help that turns out to be no help. The elves understand these responses not as tricks but as the land's natural self-regulation. **Respect the wood, wood respects you** is the canonical Feywood ethics.

The elves themselves have a sense of humor that realm visitors often miss at first — dry, observational, long-running, layered in the elven tongue.

## Access

Through the Feywood Border. Sanctioned entry — [[Hall of Guides]] guide, elven welcome-token, or formal diplomatic protocol — is reliable. Unsanctioned entry produces consequences documented at the Border.

**Lodging.** Visitors do not lodge "in the Feywood" generally; they lodge at specific locations ([[Vaelmere]] for most realm visitors, [[Thornveil]] for higher-level diplomatic visits, occasionally noble family estates).

**Rights and protections.** Feywood laws and customs apply within Feywood territory. Realm law does not apply in the Feywood. Realm visitors agree to this when they cross the Border. The realm does not intervene if a realm visitor runs into trouble within the Feywood.

**Exit.** Proper exit through the Border, accompanied by guide or host. Improper exit means emerging somewhere along the [[Heartwood]]'s root network at the Feywood's selection rather than the traveler's. Full canon in [[Heartwood]].
