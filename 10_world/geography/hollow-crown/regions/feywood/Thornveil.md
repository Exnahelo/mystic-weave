# Thornveil

The elven capital city of the [[Feywood]], grown around the [[Heartwood]] at the [[Feywood]]'s sacred center. Thornveil is neither a built city in the realm's sense nor a wild settlement — it is something specific to elven civilization, a place where ancient trees and centuries-old elven craftwork have grown together into a living urban whole. Homes, halls, and workshops are integrated with the forest canopy rather than clearing it; streets wind through groves rather than following geometric plans; noble family estates occupy the older growth at the city's deeper reaches; the [[Council of Elders]]' chambers hold deliberations that can last decades.

**Several thousand elves live here permanently**, joined by rare non-elf residents whose integration has been earned over years. Thornveil exists for one reason: **to protect the [[Heartwood]] at its center, and through that protection, to preserve the [[Feywood]] itself**.

## Scene texture

A traveler approaching Thornveil through the [[Feywood]] sees the city emerge from the forest rather than announcing itself against it. **There are no walls. There are no gates. There is no clear boundary where wilderness ends and city begins.** The forest gradually becomes more intentional — subtle signs of cultivation appear, paths become more defined, occasional buildings become visible among the trees — and at some point the traveler realizes they are no longer in wild Feywood terrain but are walking through Thornveil's outer reaches.

The transition is organic because the city itself is organic. Elven architecture does not impose geometric form on the forest; it works with the forest's existing character to produce structures that **feel grown rather than built**. Buildings use living trees as structural supports. Wooden frames incorporate the natural bend of branches that have been guided over decades rather than cut to specific dimensions. Walls are sometimes living plant material — woven, trained, grown into density — rather than inert timber. Roofs incorporate the canopy above, with buildings' upper floors sometimes indistinguishable from the surrounding tree structure.

**Materials.** Forest materials. Living wood. Dressed stone (carefully quarried from specific Feywood sites in ways that the land has sanctioned over generations). Woven vines and fibers. Occasional worked metal — primarily for precision tools and specialty craft rather than construction. Glass, rare and expensive, in specific windows where light quality matters.

**Light.** The city lies beneath continuous Feywood canopy, so direct sunlight is filtered and dappled throughout daylight hours. **Elven lanterns** — small crystal-and-wood lamps that glow with a soft bioluminescent light — provide gentle illumination at night. First-time realm visitors often remark on how much **quieter** the light is than in realm cities; no hard sun-shadows, no glare, no dramatic contrasts.

**Paths.** Not streets in the realm sense. Cleared ways between buildings and groves, surfaced with packed earth and occasional fitted stones, curving through the city's organic geography. **There is no central thoroughfare, no main market square in the realm-city pattern.** Movement through Thornveil involves learning its geography rather than reading its plan.

**Sounds.** Layered and peaceful. Birdsong continuous from the canopy. The soft sound of elven voices — elves speak more quietly than realm species on average. Distant sounds of craftwork, training-exercises from [[Greenshield]] practice grounds, low rhythmic chant of druidic observance from small neighborhood shrines.

The **[[Heartwood]] itself is visible from most of Thornveil's interior areas**. Its massive canopy rises above the city's average rooflines, providing both a constant visual landmark and a reliable navigation reference. Elves orient casually to the [[Heartwood]] the way realm residents orient to cardinal directions — without conscious effort, continuously.

## Districts

Five functional zones. Each is its own location node:

### [[Heartwood Approach]]

The innermost zone, immediately surrounding the [[Heartwood]]'s clearing. Buildings here are the oldest in the city. The [[Council of Elders]]' primary chambers are in the Heartwood Approach. Houses whose lineage traces back thousands of years hold residences here, including [[House Heartwood]]'s political-ceremonial seat (distinct from [[Heartwood Estate]] in the Outer Old-Growth Wild).

Routine [[Council of Elders|Council]] deliberation occurs in these grove chambers; matters of true land-weight descend to the **Council Chamber beneath the [[Heartwood]]**, accessed through the buttress roots.

### [[Noble Groves]]

Around the Heartwood Approach. Each noble family maintains specific grove-territory — a grouping of ancient trees, grown residences, working halls, and garden spaces held by the family over generations. Several dozen noble families hold recognized groves. Boundaries are known rather than marked.

The [[Greenshield]] operational headquarters is also in the Noble Groves, occupying traditional military-family estates that have served this function for generations.

### [[Common Hearth]]

The largest functional zone — the general elven population's residential and social life. Most Thornveil residents live here: craftfolk, ordinary [[Greenshield]] members between rotations, junior druids, traders, scholars. Includes residential clusters, communal gathering spaces, scattered markets (no central one in the realm-city pattern), taverns and halls, schools and training sites.

### [[Crafthall]]

Dedicated to working crafts — weaving, metalwork, woodworking (by elven methods that coax wood rather than cutting it), alchemy, and the specialty crafts that feed the elven export economy. Workshops larger and more institutionally organized than the [[Common Hearth]]'s home-workshops; specific craft traditions preserved through formal training lineages.

### [[Outer Bounds]]

The city's outermost zone, where Thornveil gradually dissolves back into [[Feywood]] wilderness. Newer residents — integrated non-elves, elves returning from long absences, younger elves establishing their own lives — often settle in the Outer Bounds first. Contains the trade-staging area for goods bound for the [[Feywood Border]] exchange windows.

## The Council of Elders

[[Thornveil]]'s governing body. Consensus-based and slow-paced.

**Composition.** Roughly thirty to fifty members, drawn from the oldest noble families, senior druidic practitioners, recognized elder figures, and specific rotating seats held by senior [[Greenshield]] commanders and [[Heartwarden]] captains. Total Council size varies; not fixed.

**Meeting chambers.** Primary chambers in [[Heartwood Approach]]. For matters of exceptional weight, the Council relocates to the [[Heartwood]] itself.

**Cadence.** Plenary sessions several times per elven year; subcommittees meet more frequently. **Decisions of weight often move through multiple iterations over months or years before final adoption.** Elders consider this pace appropriate to their timescale and to the land's.

**Decision style.** Consensus-based. Formal votes are rare; when they occur, they are understood as failures of proper deliberation.

**Diplomatic implication.** Realm diplomats who interact with the Council regularly develop specific strategies for operating across the timescale mismatch. **Skilled diplomats bring proposals years in advance; unskilled ones press for answers the Council will not produce on their schedule.**

## The Greenshield

Operational headquarters in the [[Noble Groves]].

**Structure.** The [[Greenshield]] maintains standing units organized along traditional elven military lines: ranger-archers, heavy-foot formations (elves in full war-gear are physically imposing), scout units, healer-wardens. Ranks run from common soldier through sergeant-equivalent, captain, commander, and senior commander.

The **Greenshield Commander** is the senior military officer (Tier-3 generative role), responsible for overall military readiness and coordination with the [[Council of Elders|Council]].

**Training.** All [[Greenshield]] members train continuously across their very long lives. Training sites include the main city grounds, outer training areas, and specific noble-family training groves.

**Deployment.** The [[Greenshield]] has deployed to active combat **exactly once in recorded history: the [[Discordant War]]**. Not large in numerical terms but decisive in quality. **Elven fighters are individually formidable in ways realm species find difficult to describe after witnessing them** — combination of elven longevity, continuous training, magical affinity, and Feywood-fostered connection to land and movement produces warriors whose combat capability exceeds what realm standards predict.

## Heartwardens in the city

The [[Heartwarden]] order trains at the [[Silent Grove]]. Members return to Thornveil regularly for rotations at the [[Heartwood]], for [[Council of Elders|Council]] appearances, for personal life (most have families and dwellings in Thornveil), and for the broader social reality of being recognized members of the elven community.

The **Heartwarden Captain** is the senior [[Heartwarden]] figure (Tier-3 generative role). Unlike the [[Greenshield]] Commander, whose authority is military-institutional, the [[Heartwarden]] Captain's authority is **honor-based** — first among the order's elders, selected by internal consensus among active [[Heartwarden]]s.

[[Heartwarden]]s in the city are recognizable but not flamboyantly so. Their distinctive markings — a specific woven pattern in their clothing, a particular brooch or armband, the way they carry themselves — are visible to those who know what to look for.

## The coming-of-age trial tradition

Certain noble families maintain a coming-of-age tradition: a child born into such a family is taken into the wilds to be raised closer to the land than ordinary [[Thornveil]] upbringing. When the child comes of age — roughly the elven equivalent of 60-80 years old — the family sends them into the [[Feywood]]'s outer terrain for trials. The trials test connection to the land, practical survival skill, combat capability, judgment, and moral character across weeks or months of sustained exposure.

**Not all candidates survive the trials.** This is considered acceptable by traditional families. The [[Feywood]] does not moderate its responses to youth, and the families consider this honesty part of the training's value.

Roughly a dozen families hold the tradition, each with its own specific trial-style. [[House Heartwood]]'s variant — wild-upbringing followed by Proving at 90 — is canonical (see [[Heartwood Estate]]).

## Integrated non-elf residents

A small population of non-elven residents lives in Thornveil, mostly in the [[Outer Bounds]]. Individuals — humans, rare dwarves, occasional drakari, and a few of other ancestries — who have earned their place through years or decades of demonstrated respect and integration with the elven community.

**Integration is not formalized.** No single authority grants it; no ceremony marks it. It becomes real through accumulated consensus of the elves who know the individual. Rarely takes less than a decade in elven timescales; often longer.

The [[Dewhollow]] community in the realm includes several individuals who have Thornveil-resident family members or who maintain professional working relationships with Thornveil residents.

## Character

**Elven daily life runs at a different pace than human daily life.** Conversations are longer. Decisions are slower. Relationships are measured in centuries rather than years. A visit to a friend might last an afternoon or a week; either is ordinary. Children mature over what realm humans would consider multiple generations.

Visitors often find Thornveil **peaceful in a way that feels like arrival rather than tourism**. Realm cities have specific energies — [[Drakenvale City]]'s civic formality, [[Dracelune]]'s liminal vivacity, [[Deephollow]]'s underground working rhythm. Thornveil's energy is none of these; a quieter presence that reshapes the visitor's sense of time while they remain in it.

Elven conversation with realm visitors carries distinctive qualities. Elves listen carefully, including pauses that realm speakers sometimes find uncomfortable. Responses are considered rather than quick. Humor is present but subtle. Elves are genuinely interested in realm visitors but find realm-side pacing exhausting over extended conversations.

## Access

Accessible only through the [[Feywood]] interior, typically with an elven host or a [[Hall of Guides]] guide. **Direct approach from the [[Feywood Border]] is not possible in ordinary experience** — the [[Feywood]]'s geometry does not extend a direct path from the Border to Thornveil for travelers who haven't earned one.

Typical sanctioned visitors:
- Realm Council-level delegations on diplomatic business
- Scholars with [[Council of Elders|Feywood Council]] research permissions
- [[Silver Scale Trading Company|SSTC]] or realm-trade representatives meeting with the Council's Trade Envoys on extended matters
- Family members visiting integrated non-elf residents

**Dragon Guard presence: None.** Thornveil is a sovereign city; the realm's military does not operate within it.

**Lodging.** Visitors stay as guests in specific elven hosts' estates (the common arrangement for diplomats and scholars), at small guest residences maintained by the Council for short-term visitors, or with integrated non-elf residents who have the capacity to host.

**Duration.** Visits typically run several days minimum due to travel time through the [[Feywood]].
